Earlier this year I received a significant grant from the Wallenberg Foundation. It is the kind of thing that warrants a proper explanation — not just a LinkedIn announcement — because the questions it funds are ones I have been circling for a while.

## The research

The grant supports work on private equity payout policy: specifically, whether the cash that flows out of PE-backed firms represents value creation or value extraction. The distinction matters more than it might seem. If payouts follow performance, they are a feature of the PE model. If they precede distress, they are closer to a bug — and a socially costly one.

The 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act created a natural experiment worth exploiting here. Changes to the carried interest holding period created differential incentives across fund vintages in ways that were largely predetermined by fund structure. That kind of variation is rare in private markets research, where endogeneity is usually the dominant methodological headache.

## What I will actually do with it

The honest answer is: mostly data. Private markets data is expensive, fragmented, and inconsistently reported. A meaningful portion of the grant goes toward building a dataset that is worth having — one that links fund-level financing decisions to portfolio company outcomes in a way that existing commercial databases do not do well.

The remainder supports time. Time to think carefully about identification rather than rushing to a result.

## A note on public writing

One thing I want to do differently this year is write more publicly about work in progress — not the results, which change, but the questions and the methods. Academic publishing is slow. The gap between having a thought and putting it in a journal is often two or three years. This feels like the right venue to close some of that gap.
